# Into the Minkhole — narrative outline and production script

[Plan](plan.md) · [Visual guide](visual-guide.md) · [Inputs](inputs.md)

Creative brief — 24 November 2026. Working script v4, approximately 2–3 minutes. This is the presenter-led demo and finished short, not a new engineering plan, release gate or claim of completed capabilities.

## Creative proposition

Show what happens when a creator retains creative direction and feedback but leaves the technical implementation to agents. Use the recognizable red-pill/blue-pill scene from The Matrix as a familiar starting point, and transform it into a coherent, playful short.

The delivery should feel like a developer sharing an experiment, not a YouTube personality performing a stunt or a conventional AI launch. The production can be polished; the narration should remain natural and understated.

## Magic-trick structure and pacing

The outer trick: familiar source footage (pledge) → agent-led transformation (turn) → finished short (prestige). The inner trick: familiar choice → unexpected emotional dilemma → spectacular change of medium.

| Approx. time | Beat | Pace and audience payoff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00–0:22 | The pledge | Show the source quickly; establish the ambitious remix and the explicit human/agent division of labour. |
| 0:22–0:36 | Difficulty | Rapid visual buildup of interconnected creative problems. |
| 0:36–0:53 | Rough cut | First small payoff: cheap robotic voice over an unmoving mouth. Let the awkwardness land. |
| 0:53–1:18 | Machinery and iteration | Fast, readable, real evidence; one mini before/feedback/after demonstration. |
| 1:18–1:33 | Assembly and pack | Decelerate, show the coherent timeline, briefly explain the pack, then pause. |
| 1:33–2:15 | Prestige | Play the finished film uninterrupted. Deadpan setup, slow guilt beat, explosive pixel adventure. |
| 2:15–2:40 | Meaning and invitation | Return to the presenter, explain the shared foundation, invite feedback. |

These are proposed editing targets, not measured voiceover timings. Adjust once the narration and cut exist.

## 01 — The demo

### The experiment

**Direction:** Show the recognisable original scene immediately, alongside a brief introduction to Astrid.

**Narration:**

> Hey, so I’ve been building an open-source creative tool called Astrid. The idea is to give people and agents a way to push what’s creatively possible with open models.
>
> Rather than try to explain how it works, I thought I’d show you an example of how I use it. I’m going to remix the famous red pill, blue pill scene from The Matrix, changing the characters, environments, dialogue and more, while keeping everything consistent.

### Why it’s difficult

**Direction:** Pick up the pace: show the shot breakdown, character references, model workflows and timeline.

**Narration:**

> My role in this will be purely creative direction and feedback. I’m not going to be involved in how any of this is actually done. I’m going to leave the how to the agents.
>
> There are a lot of little interconnected problems to solve: mapping out the sequence, writing the script, designing the characters, generating the clips and sound, and editing it all into a coherent piece.

The human supplies intent, taste and feedback; the agent chooses the technical approach, tools and implementation. This is the condition of the magic trick.

### Work out the scene first

**Direction:** Show the request, original footage and deliberately rough first cut. Let the audience hear the actual placeholder voice.

**Narration:**

> I start by asking Astrid to find the original footage and describing what I want to happen. We work out the shots and put together a really low-fidelity version using the original clips, some basic mock-up images and a crappy voiceover, just to get the sequence right.

**First small payoff:** Hard cut to the original guide with his mouth unmoving. A cheap, flat system TTS voice says, “You have two options.” Hold for a beat, then cut back to the process without explaining the joke. The roughness is funny and establishes distance between the starting point and the final result. Do not reveal the new dialogue yet.

### Generate, review, repeat

**Direction:** Speed up: show the agent choosing approaches, shot variants and one real before-and-after feedback example. Withhold the mink’s sad moment, the pixel transformation and the magical world.

**Narration:**

> Once we’ve got that right, the agent figures out which approach to take for each shot. Some need inpainting, some need video continuation, some just need new dialogue or sound, and some are completely new shots. It sends the results back to me for feedback, and we iterate until they work.
>
> Then it edits everything together, and we go through two rounds of feedback on the edit.

**Miniature magic trick:** Show a real intermediate shot, the actual creative note (e.g. “Make him look more annoyed”), and the resulting revision. Keep the example short and concrete. Show the agent making the technical decision rather than the presenter doing it. Capture actual intermediate versions, selected approaches, tools, audio and timeline changes. Do not invent evidence.

### The broader idea

**Direction:** Slow down. Briefly show the final timeline and the pack. Pause after the final line, then cut to black and the entrance animation.

**Narration:**

> This all uses an Astrid pack I’ve been working on. This one’s focused on remixing existing scenes, but you can build your own packs on top of ComfyUI workflows, code or other tools.
>
> And here’s the final product.

## 02 — The choice (the finished short)

Play the finished scene uninterrupted. Retain the gravity of the original setup, but replace the protagonist with a new character. The humour comes from the solemn guide, the irritated protagonist and the excitable little pixel-art mink.

**00 — Entrance.** On black, the Astrid wordmark appears. The mink jumps on top, then leaps forward. Match cut to the guide’s glasses: the mink flies out of a lens towards us, revealing the scene. Keep this ident to two or three seconds.

**01 — The setup.** The guide sits opposite the protagonist, offering a green pill with complete seriousness.

GUIDE: “You take the green pill, you keep running workflows by hand, tuning parameters, trying to get everything to work together.”

**02 — The alternative.** The guide opens his other hand. A tiny pixel-art mink bounces around in his palm, desperate for attention.

GUIDE: “Or you take this cute little pixely creature, and you see just how deep the mink hole goes.”

**03 — The obvious choice.** The protagonist looks at the mink, confused and visibly unimpressed. Without much hesitation, he reaches for the green pill.

**04 — The interruption.** Just before the protagonist takes the pill, the guide adds something almost as an afterthought.

GUIDE: “Oh, by the way, I don’t really know what happens to him if you don’t pick him.”

**05 — The guilt.** The mink abruptly stops jumping. Its ears droop. A tiny disappointed squeak, then an uncomfortable silence. The protagonist looks from the pill to the mink.

**06 — The reversal.** The protagonist sighs, withdraws his hand from the pill and reaches for the mink instead.

PROTAGONIST: “Oh, what the hell.”

**07 — The transformation and adventure.** The moment he touches the mink, the protagonist turns pixelated, with the effect spreading from his hand across his body. The two spring upwards into a colourful, magical pixel-art world, flying through strange landscapes and into the sky. The mink is delighted; the protagonist remains a little annoyed and bewildered.

**08 — End card.** The mink flies towards the camera and transforms into the Astrid logo. Let the film finish on the logo before returning to the presenter.

### Performance and sound

Keep the guide deadpan and the protagonist slightly irritated, even in pixel form. Hold the sad-mink beat until it feels awkward. Use serious ambience, a tiny squeak, silence, then a euphoric burst of sound and colour. The entrance and end card are bookends for the short itself.

The transformation should exceed the audience's expectation of a simple scene remix. The reluctant choice results in an entire new visual medium. Do not explain the metaphor or spoil it during the walkthrough.

## 03 — The closing

**Direction:** Return to the presenter or the editor. Keep this brief; show the website as the invitation to try it.

**Narration:**

> So that’s Astrid. The idea is to create a shared foundation for agents that people can build higher-level tools and guidance on top of, so we can combine all these different pieces of the open-source ecosystem to do extraordinary things. Ultimately, the goal is just to help you do more and create more.
>
> All of this happens inside a creative editor as well, and I’ll be sharing more of the app very soon. If you’d like to try it out, head to astrid.haus. Any feedback is hugely appreciated.

## Editorial guardrails

- Keep all narration provisional until it can describe the actual agent-led process truthfully. If the intended two editing feedback rounds or other steps change, update the narration.
- Use actual prompts, agent actions, intermediate results and editor captures; make genuine progress visible, not synthetic progress displays.
- Preserve the reveal: no complete finished scene, sad-mink beat, transformation, magical world or final sound design in the technical montage.
- Confirm rights to any source footage or audio used in the public version.
- The narrative is a production target, not evidence that Plan 59 or any product release has achieved it.
