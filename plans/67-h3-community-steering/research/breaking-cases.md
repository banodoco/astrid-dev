# What creators will ask H3 levers to do

**Verdict.** Keep the three families and the one-pass job. Change two claims before handoff. Six mechanisms describe the planned compiler, but an extension can depend on several of them. A skill can coordinate passes, but it can reproduce a workflow only if each pass exports what the next one consumes. Neither finding warrants a fourth family or a general graph editor.

Rank reflects likely editorial impact, frequency of the *ask*, and how easily a plausible request can silently do the wrong thing. “Common” and “specialist” are judgments, not usage statistics. Community workflows establish that people build these graphs. Source inspection establishes wiring and limits. Neither establishes render quality.

Two further comparisons sharpen the ranking. LTX documents distinct retake, keyframe and two-stage pipelines. “Redo this interval” can be a single creative instruction while the backend chooses a specific operation with specific inputs. In H3, the OneClick Swap author removes the original scene reference for an insertion into empty space because the reference can encourage the model to reproduce that empty space. The combination is legal. A skill can flag the contrary instructions and show the tracked region without making overlap a compiler error. ## Decisions for Plans 64–66

1. **Plan 64 SR-1/2/3.** Preserve the one-seed, one-reference-set rule and the existing effective reference rows. At admission, compare the requested scope of each grant with the actual scope of its bindings. Report which occurrence and soundtrack each tag identifies. Merely finding the requested ordinal in the prompt leaves a wrong-but-valid binding undetected. Stable IDs in templates can resolve to the existing stock tags.
2. **Plan 65 DS-2/3 and Plan 66 CC-3/8.** Reuse the existing conform manifest and raster. State what exactness is measured against. Keep model protection, delivered paste and conform transform distinct in the resolved view. Name both occurrences when grants or stills conflict after quantization. No new canvas grammar is needed.
3. **Plan 66 CC-1/2/3.** Describe six current merge destinations. Permit a lowering to declare dependencies, required artifacts and interactions with other destinations. Resolve and validate those dependencies once. Maintain separate claims for syntax accepted, graph compiled, combination exercised and creative result observed. A template’s presence alone supplies no render evidence. This preserves the existing decision to check whether bridge/insert/prefix preparations are equivalent before consolidating them.
4. **Plan 66 CC-4/6/7a/9.** Keep timed text soft, the retained-guide disclosure, and the named prefix-edit refusal. Teach the locked-song and staged-prefix examples alongside redubbing. The document exercise got all five structures right in round two at 4.2/10 reported confidence; the combined continuation still exceeds CC-7a. Include an expected refusal or decomposition in the cold-read rubric. Current task cards already include timed prompts; the older recommendation to reserve them is stale. Use shipped field names and distinguish unavailable control/phase support. Haiku exercise
5. **After Plan 66.** Scope take selection, dependency tracking, hard budget enforcement and optional typed artifact exchange as shot/workflow work. Record the intended consumer and compatibility requirements before promising latent retention or sampler resume. Leave stem mixing, layer compositing and original-master delivery to finishing tools. These boundaries keep normal still, reference, guide and redub requests short.
