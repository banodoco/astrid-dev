# Creative units — the living planet

[Plan](plan.md) · [Tasks and status](tasks.md) · [Shared art direction](art-direction.md) · [References](assets/README.md)

## Why this is its own production layer

Building a terrain system, a mink rig and a clock adapter are **shared technical tasks**. Building the tea clearing or the tiny glider is **creative direction work**. Each piece needs its own visual identity and taste judgment, but not necessarily its own general-purpose architecture, review ritual or fixed one-day engineering limit.

Use the production's **individual scene or flying object** as the unit of artistic acceptance. Its implementation may be several practical engineering packets, or just one small one. The task ledger in [tasks.md](tasks.md) is the only progress tracker; this document is the creative brief and reusable acceptance contract.

## The scene card — minimum complete creative delivery

Every SC card has, before detailed production: a one-paragraph emotional purpose, an approximate place/latitude and accessible time-of-day views on the globe, a quick silhouette/graybox, character and prop scale relative to the planet, one distinctive activity or deliberately chosen still moment, what it should *not* copy, and an intentional relationship to nearby empty terrain.

Every completed SC card includes:
- An actual Vision-hero-size screenshot at **390×844 and 1440×900**, with relevant typography and cloud layers; also daylight and night views.
- A short **8–20-second** clip showing its defining motion and rest, or an accepted intentionally static beat. The founding workshop vertical slice is longer, **20–30 seconds**, because it establishes the shared animation grammar.
- Curvature, correct terrain contact, materials, occlusion, coherent lighting and mascot identity. No oversized objects, excessive constant motion or competing focal points.
- Source revision/asset provenance and a recorded **accepted / revise / omit** verdict by the accountable creative judge. An omitted concept needs a documented curation decision, not silent disappearance. Post evidence and 2–3 tailored optional questions to the owner after every unit; do not wait for a response.

Graybox → on-page art look → motion/material finish → integration approval. Reuse the shared model, animation and lighting systems; don't build bespoke engines for each little joke.

## Eight scene concepts to art-direct

The first scene establishes the grammar; the other seven are distinct art units. These eight are a **proposed creative portfolio**, grounded in the reference ideas discussed for Astrid, not a command to arrange eight equally prominent objects across the visible face.

| Scene | Emotional beat and composition | Signature moment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **SC-01: Collaborating makers** | Warm community invention around a tiny shared workshop, with two or three small minks | One passes/steadies a part while another adjusts it; they pause and look at the result |
| **SC-02: Tea clearing** | Companionship, modest props, relaxed curved bodies | The pour, wait, sip and glance, with a tiny humorous hesitation |
| **SC-03: Circling meadow** | Playfulness and lively counterpoint in an open patch | One of three runners gets distracted; the others respond asymmetrically |
| **SC-04: Rose keeper** | Solitude on a lightly sculpted sandy stretch; preserve negative space | One tender watering/checking gesture followed by stillness |
| **SC-05: Mystery object** | A playful puzzle tucked near a grove/path; original Astrid styling | One character inspects the box/object, receives a tiny response, investigates again |
| **SC-06: Astronomer** | A curious observer on a quiet raised patch | Telescope adjustment, a notebook mark, a look back towards the sky |
| **SC-07: Meditating elder** | Stillness on a distant ridge or beside a lone tree | Minimal breathing / leaf movement, not an exaggerated joke |
| **SC-08: Cozy creator** | Intimate lamplit workstation, subtle node-graph pixel details | Brief focus, tiny change on the screen, delighted attention |

The meaning of these scenes is more important than prop count. Translate all cultural references into original Astrid shapes and styling; do not reproduce another creator's character, logo, game asset or image. Design the *far side* of the globe with the same care as the hero-facing side.

## Four airborne creative units

Each FL concept has **its own** silhouette, scale, route and altitude, motion cadence, visibility frequency, plausible occlusion around the planet, day/night lighting, and short signed-off proof clip. Like scenes, a flight can be accepted, revised or omitted; the owner decides whether the full set belongs in the finished composition.

| Flying unit | Creative direction and distinction |
| --- | --- |
| **FL-01: Bird flock** | A small irregular flock; natural distance and a brief formation change |
| **FL-02: Tiny glider** | One recognisable handmade flying device with a graceful, slow pass |
| **FL-03: Balloon traveller** | Buoyant, gentle floating motion and a tiny tactile payload |
| **FL-04: Original airborne oddity** | A fourth concept chosen from a small art exploration—perhaps pixel-light creatures or a curious floating machine |

No item should feel like a full-screen film prop. Flying objects move independently of the **24-hour sky clock**: no hour updates and no planet yaw changes. Stagger their activity; showing four things in flight simultaneously may be wrong.

## Two distinct cinematic treatments

**CAM-IN:** Flying camera approach with a considered arc, settling at the *current-time* hemisphere, while planet yaw and selected time remain unchanged. Hero text is readable immediately. The scene cannot briefly disappear during loading.

**CAM-OUT:** A connected retreat/arc or carefully designed dissolve back to Home, supporting interrupted navigation and immediate response, while preserving the same effective sky hour. Reduced motion skips spatial camera travel for both treatments.

The entry/exit animations are each art-directed and accepted independently. Their shared choreography is reviewed once against the completed world and flight pieces.

## Combined globe and airspace acceptance

The final 360° tour and fixed-hour shots at **00:00, 06:00, 12:00 and 18:00** should show a coherent inhabited sphere with distinct experiences distributed across longitudes, generous quiet stretches, no accidental crowded facade, correct silhouette, and no jump when rotating. An independent final art pass judges the **whole**, not just the acceptance of each piece. The combined aerial pass ensures no object competes with the headline, camera movement, important scenes or other flyers.

**Creative review cadence:** the creative manager/independent reviewer accepts every SC/FL/CAM unit against its own art criteria and shares screenshots, motion and 2–3 meaningful questions after each. Ordinary per-unit feedback is **optional** and never waits on the owner. The four [load-bearing creative decisions](chatgpt-orchestration.md#feedback-routine-progress-vs-human-decision)—VG-STYLE, VG-STORY, VG-WORLD and VG-MOTION—**do** hold dependent artistic commitments until the owner answers; independent engineering and unrelated art exploration continue. Show useful 2–3 scene batches and the full globe; accept delayed feedback as scoped rework, not automatic undo. The owner may interrupt, redirect or pause at any time and explicitly controls final publication.
