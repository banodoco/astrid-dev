**Disposition:** genuine conflict between two stated guarantees; no safe, source-supported product patch identified. Needs the timeline contract owner to decide the corrupt-history boundary. ## Source contract and chronology

* Commit `3021a5b79102e9c0873f70df46f5a686bd1276ff` (20 October) added the old-byte-corruption exact-retry assertion. Its message says selected media bytes must be rechecked while preserving ledger-first retries and zero-write refusal on corruption. Its production diff only added selected-object verification in `timeline_commit_port.py` (current `:1058-1060`); it did not change accepted-history verification. The old/new fixture originally deduplicated identical bytes, so that test did not isolate the old-history versus new-selection boundary.
* Normative `contract/commit-v2.md:254-264` (dated 22 October at `:1-9`) says an authorized exact retry returns the durable winner without rerunning effects or adding history, and 503 `not_ready` denotes an unknown outcome. The same contract says verified closure, CAS and history chain remain authoritative (`:35-41`) and historical receipts must remain authentic with every other authentication check intact (`:25-33`). It does not expressly resolve an accepted retry when a retained object needed to authenticate that winner has become corrupt.
* Later commit `43407a1820116b73b4db014b938872534eeeeb5f` (25 October) introduced the current `_evaluation` exit check and `_check_history_objects`, expressly rechecking physical CAS bytes outside SQLite's change boundary, including cached ancestry. Its `tests/test_timeline_history_state.py:431-449` requires a cached legacy closure to refuse after its CAS bytes are corrupted and clear verified state. This is a deliberate integrity guard, not a general availability failure or an accidental provider invocation. `timeline_commit_store.py:654-720` also documents that no internal history entry escapes before live predicates, replay proof, and repeated physical-object input checks pass.
* `tests/test_timeline_commit_acceptance.py:753-801` proves a narrower ledger-first case: an original retry survives corruption in a **later** acceptance's event, while retrying that corrupt later acceptance yields `CommitOutcomeUnknown`. This distinguishes unrelated later corruption from corruption in the winner's own necessary ancestry. It supports fail-closed replay for dependent history.

## Judgment and user impact

The K1b expectation is a legitimate earlier acceptance test, but satisfying it by skipping retained-object validation would contradict the later explicit history-integrity implementation and its test. From these primary sources, there is no established authorization to return an authenticated accepted result whose required old media bytes fail SHA-256. Equally, the contract's unconditional exact-retry wording and `3021a5b` assertion do not authorize silently downgrading the test to 503. This is an unresolved product-contract conflict, not evidence that the selected-media check or the pinned Agent is defective.

For an authorized editor retrying after a lost acknowledgement, corruption of an old source can leave a valid durable acceptance in the ledger but produce repeated 503s until that CAS object is repaired. No duplicate edit or receipt is created on the observed path. Returning a purportedly verified receipt without its historical dependency proof risks falsely authenticating altered history; bypassing authorization, provider readiness, or physical CAS checks is not a permissible fix.

## Required owner decision and minimal follow-up

The timeline contract owner must choose one explicit rule for **exact retry after corruption of a CAS object in the accepted winner's own history**:

1. **Integrity refusal governs** (most consistent with the later D94 guard): specify a stable, fail-closed public outcome for this condition, clarify that exact replay requires verifiable winner history, and revise the collision assertion only under that contract decision. Preserve a separate clean-history exact-retry assertion and all no-duplicate/no-write checks. Whether the current generic 503 is the intended public status and retry advice also needs an explicit decision; perpetual exact retry cannot repair corrupt bytes.
2. **Receipt replay governs despite corrupt history:** define and implement an independently authenticated durable receipt proof that does not require the missing/corrupt old CAS dependency, while retaining full verification for history/closure operations and fresh commits. No such proof or scoped exception is evident in this tree. Do not implement this as an early ledger return or skipped `_check_history_objects`.

After the decision, proof should select both atomic IDs, the clean replay and corruption cases in `tests/test_timeline_clip_replacement.py`, `tests/test_timeline_history_state.py::test_cached_legacy_closure_still_checks_physical_cas_bytes`, and `tests/test_timeline_commit_acceptance.py::test_original_retry_ignores_corrupt_later_acceptance`. Check receipt/event/ledger counts, unchanged head, no provider execution on exact retries, same-key changed-body mismatch, authorization before CAS reads, and restored-CAS recovery. Do not treat the present two failing tests as green or remove their assertions without the owner rule.
