Lane dispatch was written by hand each time, and it contradicted itself. Eight dispatch-like sections ([releases/13/generation.md](../../releases/13/generation.md), [14](../../releases/14/run.md), [15](../../releases/15/run.md) and [16](../../releases/16/run.md) among them) repeat the same fourteen recurring fields (branch rules, routes and modes, the mailbox, standing rulings, the in-flight cap, startup checks and so on), and later sections say "works exactly like" an earlier one. Sequencing across plans was written three ways, and work spanning releases was recorded with "superseded" notes rather than one record. No template or check covers any of it: `templates/release/` has only `run.md` and `packet.md`, and the orchestrator role refers to "dispatch notes in tasks.md", which do not exist ([agent/roles/orchestrator.md](../../agent/roles/orchestrator.md)). The role documents also disagree in eleven places: what a lane is, who answers for it, whether a lane orchestrator may take the Lead's part, the default manager type, who writes `Run start` and who sends the launch, where rulings live, who advises, merge rights, duplicated rules and dangling references. The H3 lane was improvised by hand in [releases/16/briefs/h3.md](../../releases/16/briefs/h3.md), before any lane template existed; the lane brief design now uses it as its worked example.
