Release 12 took about a day for several reasons. The base's failing tests were first measured at the end, so 323 pre-existing failures had to be told apart from 40 regressions by hand, with a regression rule worked out on the fly and corrected once. The end gate ran in turn: fixes, then CI, then re-pins, then the matrix, then review. Hung CI shards were waited on for an hour. Workers retried hard failures. An N-class manager went idle with results waiting. Scope was added mid-release. And the orchestrator ended up writing the manager's dispatch plan. The manage skill now records the baseline before the first task and defines a regression as a test that ran and passed on a baseline. It runs the end gate's stages at once, with a shakedown, and treats a CI step past one and a half times its usual time as a hang. It escalates a failure after about 20 minutes without progress. The orchestrate skill now says the orchestrator doesn't manage, picks the manager's model by the run, fixes the scope when a release starts, and chains `send_later` for check-ins faster than hourly. A new `env/baseline-diff.py` sorts the results mechanically, and its lists are committed under `plans/<project>/evidence/tests/`, so nobody sorts them by hand.
