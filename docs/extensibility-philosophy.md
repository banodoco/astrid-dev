# A collective foundation for AI-powered creative tools

We are building Astrid as a collective foundation for AI-powered creative tooling. People and their assistive agents should be able to build, use, and share their own tools with open-source AI models. The foundation should make useful work dependable while leaving room for people to shape tools around what they want to create.

## A small, dependable shared foundation

Keep shared primitives focused, composable, and reliable. Before extending the foundation, inspect what is already there and reuse current foundations where they fit. Put specialized creative behavior in packs, and improve shared primitives when several tools need the same capability. A strong foundation helps people start quickly and makes its operation clear enough to understand and change.

## Extensibility is part of every design

Every tool and every extension should be designed so it can itself be extended. Provide deliberate, documented, agent-accessible interfaces that let people and agents adapt tools during creative work, then share new tools and reusable foundations with others. We shape our tools and our tools shape us. With agents, that becomes an ongoing creative process: we reshape our tools as we work, and new possibilities in those tools change what we can imagine and make.

**[H3_AV and VibeComfy](../plans/17-vibecomfy-extensibility.md):** H3_AV provides a higher-level abstraction over MiniMax H3 workflows in VibeComfy. Improve VibeComfy’s existing foundations so people can build similar packs more easily, then make those packs open to further extension.

**[Timeline editing](../plans/18-timeline-editing.md):** a pack of dependable editing primitives becomes a foundation for custom ways to understand video, alternative views, effects, and creative workflows. People and agents can build these extensions on the same underlying material and combine them with other tools.

## People choose their level of control

People choose whether to use a complete tool or shape one part of it. They decide whether to run the whole tool locally or use selected remote services, and choose the compute, models, agents, interfaces, and access available to it. Make those choices clear, and preserve the user's intent and work when agents act on their behalf.

A good contribution makes useful creative work possible, makes tools easier to build on, or makes the shared foundation simpler to understand and trust.
