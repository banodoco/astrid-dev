## Independent judgment

Step back from technical line review. Pretend you are the Lead about to deliver this as one substantial product project. What would you change about the overall strategy?

1. Is the release outcome clear, important, independently deliverable, and the smallest coherent set of work? Are there omitted dependability issues, unnecessary features, duplicated mechanisms, needless abstraction, confused ownership or alternate routes? What can be cut, consolidated, reused or safely deferred, with source evidence? What harm remains?
2. Does the release's Approach explain a thoughtful strategy, rather than a list of work? Compare the proposed sequence with a credible alternative, including early end-to-end proof versus foundation-first and appropriate parallel delivery. What must settle before what? Identify the critical path, unjustified serialization, contract/file conflicts, actual capacity and qualification-machine constraints, and the earliest useful integrated result. Consider what can begin before a dependency lands; distinguish start/land/proof/collision/capacity. Identify risky joins, exact pin and migration/rollback ordering and what upstream changes invalidate. Are the early integrated milestones and final proof actually achievable without relying on later release work?
3. Does the strategy make life straightforward for implementers, a weaker agent and the Lead? Are the project task and lane responsibilities, proof, gates and owner decisions sufficient to run without reopening design? Can actual R15 changes be carried forward without repeating work or importing unknown public-main divergence? Are support/experimental promises and release/semantic version constraints honest, including Plan 31 required checks versus R17, and dependable saving versus R18?
4. Are all consequential findings linked to retrievable immutable original evidence and canonical decisions/tasks? Have previous disagreements and negative findings been preserved? Is there any avoidable duplicate record or process overhead? Recommend the fewest changes necessary, not another framework.
