## Outcome

- **For creators and agents:** one request describes a shot (what to keep exactly, what to generate or edit, how to steer), at whole-shot and frame level.
- **Placements, not modes:** continue, bridge and insert are placements of clips or frames on the timeline.
- **Exact means exact:** kept pixels and samples are verified in the final file.
- **The dry run tells the truth first:** before any GPU spend it states what is exact, soft, verified or untested, the cost, and the nearest option to any refusal.
- **For developers:** a new capability is one ordinary pack, a pinned node repository plus one YAML file per contribution and a hand-written skill section. It stacks with everything else through typed sockets, footprints and a test kit, with no Astrid engineering.
- **In Release 17,** one request can run several passes (de-rope, edit-then-extend, refine, repair).

## Queue

The "Execution units" tables in each plan's `tasks.md` are the queue: [Plan 66](../../../plans/66-h3-composable-core/tasks.md), [Plan 68](../../../plans/68-h3-guides-and-stills/tasks.md), [Plan 70](../../../plans/70-h3-continuation-bridges-inserts/tasks.md), [Plan 67](../../../plans/67-h3-community-steering/tasks.md), [Plan 107](../../../plans/107-h3-passes/tasks.md). Two units never write the same function at once; a shared filename alone is not a dependency. Release 16's longest chain is estimated at 19–31 working days; with Plan 107, 28–46.

## How it runs

**Option:** packets

- **Start.** See [Toolkit check at start](#toolkit-check-at-start).
- **Dispatch.** The orchestrator writes each unit's packet at dispatch from the unit's cards: outcome, May touch (the card's Scope), focused proof, base SHA and the target `release/<N>/plan-<PP>`. No packet files are prepared ahead. The older `releases/16/packets/R16-P66-*`, `-P67-*`, `-P68-*` and `-P70-*` files are history only.
- **Authors (ChatGPT route).**
  - **The chats.** One fresh normal ChatGPT chat on the web per unit, signed in to the owner's own account, in the unit's route mode: N High, X Extra High, S Pro ([modes](../../../agent/shared/run-contract.md#chatgpt-session-modes)). Never ChatGPT for Work, and never one mode for everything. T editorial work goes to a fresh Claude Opus session. The chat writes the unit, runs its focused proof in its sandbox, pushes through its GitHub connector, and posts its early STATUS, READY-PROOF or BLOCKED on the lane board.
  - **The manager** (`h3-manager`) is one Codex session in the Codex app on the owner's computer, with Chrome signed in to ChatGPT. It opens, names and resumes the chats, writes each unit's packet from its cards at dispatch, and keeps at most three chats running. The orchestrator cannot create or wake it, so:
    1. At start, and for every replacement, the orchestrator posts the manager's three-part launch line on the lane board as `LAUNCH h3-manager` and tells the owner in its STATUS. The line names the `astrid-dev-orchestrator` skill's [multi-manager dispatch](../../../agent/roles/orchestrator/multi-manager-dispatch.md) workstream, this brief, and `h3-orchestrator` as its creator.
    2. The owner pastes it into a new Codex thread. Nobody else sends it.
    3. The manager follows the recorded Codex-app steps on [#2030](https://github.com/banodoco/astrid-dev/issues/2030) (`TOOLKIT` comments): its hourly check-in automation, opening a chat and choosing its mode, and resuming a chat.
  - **The orchestrator** runs the queue: it marks units Ready on the board, answers each READY-PROOF, sends reviews, merges and replaces sessions.
  - **Fallback, at a unit boundary, said once on the board:** if no `h3-manager` STATUS appears within two hours of a LAUNCH post, or the manager stalls after one replacement, the orchestrator runs units itself as Codex workers (`codex exec`, the [guide](../../../agent/guides/codex-in-claude-cloud/README.md#use-codex-for-the-workers-under-a-claude-manager); N Luna, X Terra, S Sol; the owner opens one `codex login --device-auth` link). If that is unavailable too, it uses Claude workers by the same routes (N Haiku, X Sonnet, S Opus). It returns to the chats when a manager posts again.
- **Proof.** The author runs the focused proof, pushes `p<PP>/<unit>`, and opens a PR into the plan branch; green CI plus the shown proof is the evidence. Once Plan 66 CC-14 lands, its `engine_deps` lane is the one proof workflow for every unit and pack. A unit may land as several PRs. There is no separate proof custodian.
- **Reviews.**
  - The reviewer's ACCEPT is required at Plan 66 RS1 (job-record half, then contract half), on every S-route unit, at Plan 67 R (the programme's integrated review) and at Plan 107 R. Other units merge on green CI and shown proof.
  - Findings become rows `R<n>.<m>` under the reviewed card. They go back to the same author once, then to a fresh session.
- **Merges.**
  - The orchestrator merges `release/<N>/main` forward into each plan branch as it moves.
  - When a plan's units are merged, and its review stop accepted where it has one, it merges the plan branch into `release/<N>/main`. It does this after any overlapping merge from other lanes.
  - It cuts later branches itself with `git push` from the base named in the release brief.
- **CI.** astrid-agent CI is shared with Plans 93, 94 and 38:
  - batch several units per push;
  - keep one run per branch;
  - cancel your own superseded runs;
  - check the running `ci.yml` runs before pushing;
  - keep heavy suites on CI, never on a shared session.
- **Check-ins.**
  - The orchestrator keeps an hourly scheduled task (`create_trigger` on this session) and a keep-awake loop. The manager keeps its own hourly Codex automation. Chats only post milestones. Each check reads the lane board by cursor (`inbox.py --after <cursor>`), acts, and posts one STATUS of 10 lines or fewer: units merged, units running, blocked items with their cause, the next units, `acted on:` and `cursor:`.
  - Anyone writes to the orchestrator by posting on the lane board with `to: h3-orchestrator` and a type (REQUEST, DECISION, BLOCKED); the format is in the board's first post. It answers in the next STATUS or in a reply there.
  - Something that cannot wait an hour: a Claude session also sends `send_message` to the orchestrator's session id (in this brief's header) as a doorbell, after posting on the board. The owner can also write in the orchestrator's session directly. Nothing can wake the Codex manager or a chat: they read the board at their next check-in, and an urgent message to them goes to the owner.
  - The reviewer posts its verdicts on the lane board. The orchestrator posts to the Release 16 Lead only on the Lead's board, and only for release-level questions.
- **Skills** are hand-written editorial guides, mixing instruction and creative inspiration, and never generated. Plan 66 CC-6 and Plan 67 CS-8 write them; each pack ships its own written section.
