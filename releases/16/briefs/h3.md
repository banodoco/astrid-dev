**Board:** `board: lane h3`, opened by the orchestrator at start and linked here in its first progress PR. It is this lane's one message board: everyone writes to the orchestrator there, and the manager and chats read only this board.

This is the one home for how the H3 programme runs. **Precedence:** for this lane, this brief wins over the orchestrator skill wherever they differ. The [run contract](../../../agent/shared/run-contract.md)'s safety, proof and permission rules win over this brief: a brief never grants more authority than the owner gave, and never weakens a check. Written 28 November 2026, ahead of the lane-brief template that [Plan 108](../../../plans/108-lane-briefs/plan.md) adds; Plan 108's H-1 conforms it to that template.

## Outcome

- **For creators and agents:** one request describes a shot (what to keep exactly, what to generate or edit, how to steer), at whole-shot and frame level.
- **Placements, not modes:** continue, bridge and insert are placements of clips or frames on the timeline.
- **Exact means exact:** kept pixels and samples are verified in the final file.
- **The dry run tells the truth first:** before any GPU spend it states what is exact, soft, verified or untested, the cost, and the nearest option to any refusal.
- **For developers:** a new capability is one ordinary pack, a pinned node repository plus one YAML file per contribution and a hand-written skill section. It stacks with everything else through typed sockets, footprints and a test kit, with no Astrid engineering.
- **In Release 17,** one request can run several passes (de-rope, edit-then-extend, refine, repair).

## How it runs

**Option:** packets

- **Start.** See [Toolkit check at start](#toolkit-check-at-start).
- **Dispatch.** The orchestrator marks a unit `READY <unit>` on the board when its Depends are merged. The manager then writes that unit's packet from its cards, into the [chat start message](#start-messages): outcome, May touch (the card's Scope), focused proof, base SHA and the target `release/<N>/plan-<PP>`. No packet files are prepared ahead. The older `releases/16/packets/R16-P66-*`, `-P67-*`, `-P68-*` and `-P70-*` files are history only.
- **Authors (ChatGPT route).**
  - **The chats.** One fresh normal ChatGPT chat on the web per unit, signed in to the owner's own account, in the unit's route mode: N High, X Extra High, S Pro ([modes](../../../agent/shared/run-contract.md#chatgpt-session-modes)). Never ChatGPT for Work, and never one mode for everything. T editorial work goes to a fresh Claude Opus session. Each chat starts from the [chat start message](#start-messages) and returns the way it says.
  - **The manager** (`h3-manager`) is one Codex session in the Codex app on the owner's computer, with Chrome signed in to ChatGPT. It opens, names and resumes the chats, writes each Ready unit's packet, and keeps at most three chats running. It does not run proof, merge or rule; it relays those to the orchestrator on the board. It runs on Terra (route X), checks in hourly at a minute other than :17 and :30, and may replace a chat once (branch quiet 30 minutes, the head named in the new chat's first message). A second stall parks the unit for the owner. The orchestrator cannot create or wake it, so:
    1. At start, and for every replacement, the orchestrator posts the [manager launch](#start-messages) on the lane board as `LAUNCH h3-manager` and tells the owner in its STATUS.
    2. The owner pastes it into a new Codex thread. Nobody else sends it.
    3. The manager follows the recorded Codex-app steps on [#2030](https://github.com/banodoco/astrid-dev/issues/2030) (`TOOLKIT` comments): its hourly check-in automation, opening a chat and choosing its mode, and resuming a chat. #2030 records only High. The first time it chooses Extra High or Pro, it posts the exact steps as a `TOOLKIT` comment on the lane board. If a mode isn't offered, it posts BLOCKED and does not substitute another.
  - **Fallback, at a unit boundary, said once on the board:** if no `h3-manager` STATUS appears within two hours of a LAUNCH post, or the manager is silent for three hours ([liveness](../../../agent/shared/run-contract.md#coordination-boards-and-check-ins)), the orchestrator runs units itself as Codex workers (`codex exec`, the [guide](../../../agent/guides/codex-in-claude-cloud/README.md#use-codex-for-the-workers-under-a-claude-manager); N Luna, X Terra, S Sol; the owner opens one `codex login --device-auth` link). Codex counts as unavailable only once tried: the orchestrator installs it and posts the sign-in link in its reply, and a sign-in not completed by the next check-in has failed. Only then does it use Claude workers by the same routes (N Haiku, X Sonnet, S Opus), with the [Claude worker start message](#start-messages). It tells the owner their two choices: resume `h3-manager` by opening its Codex thread and sending "Check in now: read the lane board by cursor and post STATUS", or do nothing and let the fallback run. It returns to the chats when a manager posts again.
- **Baseline.** Before 66-U1 (whose Depends says `baseline`), the orchestrator records the test inventory on the starting versions: the hosted CI result on each plan branch at its cut SHA, starting one run per branch if none exists ([when a run is load-bearing](../../../agent/shared/run-contract.md#when-a-test-run-is-load-bearing)). It posts `BASELINE <plan> <sha> <run link>` on the board. That post is the "refreshed 59/64/65 baseline" Plan 66's critical path starts from, and it satisfies `baseline`.
- **Proof.** Each author commits its own work on `task/<PP>/<unit>` and returns the way its [start message](#start-messages) says; branches and merges follow [the pipeline](../../../agent/shared/pipeline.md#branches-and-pull-requests). No CI runs on these branches. The proof is the orchestrator's own run of the unit's focused tests, by id, on the merged tree, before it pushes the plan branch: in its container with `scripts/worker-env/`, or through a Codex worker. H3 tests need [environment setup](../../../env/README.md) that fetches other repositories, so a chat can't run them and says so. Once Plan 66 CC-14 lands, its `engine_deps` lane is the one proof workflow for every unit and pack.
- **Reviews.**
  - The reviewer's ACCEPT is required at Plan 66 RS1 (job-record half, then contract half), on every S-route unit, at Plan 67 R (the programme's integrated review) and at Plan 107 R. Other units merge on the orchestrator's proof.
  - An S-route unit's own review, of its head SHA, gates that unit's merge; a review stop after it (66-RS1a after 66-U1) is a separate review of the merged head. The orchestrator adds each finding as a row under the reviewed card in that plan's `tasks.md`, in its next progress PR: `RS1.<n>` at the RS1 stops, `R1.<m>` for a unit's own review, under its first card. They go back to the same author once, then to a fresh session.
- **Merges.**
  - The orchestrator merges `release/<N>/main` forward into each plan branch as it moves.
  - When a plan's units are merged, and its review stop accepted where it has one, the orchestrator makes the plan ready to land: it merges `release/<N>/main` forward, settles conflicts through a worker whose Scope is the conflicting files, and gets the full relevant suite green on CI on that head. It then opens the PR from the plan branch into `release/<N>/main` and posts it, `to: release <N> lead`, on the Lead's board. The Lead merges it, in order with the other lanes. Once it is merged, the orchestrator opens the plan's completion PR, ready for review, with `Completion: Yes` on the plan's own criteria; a later review stop that covers the plan (Plan 67 R) is named under Unproven, and the PR merges after it accepts.
  - The orchestrator also merges its own astrid-dev progress PRs on a green check, when they touch only this lane's plan status lines, `inputs.md` Scope decision lines, `tasks.md` rows and this brief.
  - It cuts later branches itself with `git push` from the base named in the release brief.
- **CI.** astrid-agent CI runs on PRs into `release/*/main` and pushes to it, and is shared with Plans 93, 94 and 38. The orchestrator dispatches `ci.yml` once per plan, on the plan branch's integrated head when it makes the plan ready, after checking the running `ci.yml` runs.
- **Check-ins.**
  - The orchestrator's hourly scheduled task (`create_trigger` on this session) is its wake path ([run contract](../../../agent/shared/run-contract.md#coordination-boards-and-check-ins)); the fallback clock starts at the LAUNCH post. Each check reads the lane board by cursor (`python3 agent/guides/codex-cloud/inbox.py banodoco/astrid-dev <board number> --after <cursor>`), acts, and posts one STATUS of 10 lines or fewer: units merged, units running, blocked items with their cause, the next units, `acted on:` and `cursor:`.
  - Anyone writes to the orchestrator by posting on the lane board with `to: h3-orchestrator` and a type (STATUS, READY-PROOF, REQUEST, DECISION, BLOCKED); the manager's hourly STATUS and every chat's milestones go `to: h3-orchestrator`; the format is in the board's first post, copied from the [mailbox format](../../../agent/guides/codex-cloud/README.md#establish-the-mailbox) with `run: h3-lane`. It answers in the next STATUS or in a reply there.
  - Something that cannot wait an hour: a Claude session also rings the orchestrator's session id (in this brief's header) with `send_message`, and the owner can write in its session directly. Nothing can wake the Codex manager or a chat, so an urgent message to them goes to the owner.
  - The reviewer posts its verdicts on the lane board. The orchestrator posts to the Release <N> Lead only on the Lead's board: each ready plan PR, release-level questions, and its closing note.
- **Skills** are hand-written editorial guides, mixing instruction and creative inspiration, and never generated. Plan 66 CC-6 and Plan 67 CS-8 write them; each pack ships its own written section.
