This is the one home for how the H3 programme runs. **Precedence:** for this lane, this brief wins over the orchestrator skill wherever they differ. The [run contract](../../../agent/shared/run-contract.md)'s safety, proof and permission rules win over this brief: a brief never grants more authority than the owner gave, and never weakens a check. Written 28 November 2026, ahead of the lane-brief template that [Plan 108](../../../plans/108-lane-briefs/plan.md) adds; Plan 108's H-1 conforms it to that template.

## Outcome

- **For creators and agents:** one request describes a shot (what to keep exactly, what to generate or edit, how to steer), at whole-shot and frame level.
- **Placements, not modes:** continue, bridge and insert are placements of clips or frames on the timeline.
- **Exact means exact:** kept pixels and samples are verified in the final file.
- **The dry run tells the truth first:** before any GPU spend it states what is exact, soft, verified or untested, the cost, and the nearest option to any refusal.
- **For developers:** a new capability is one ordinary pack, a pinned node repository plus one YAML file per contribution and a hand-written skill section. It stacks with everything else through typed sockets, footprints and a test kit, with no Astrid engineering.
- **In Release 17,** one request can run several passes (de-rope, edit-then-extend, refine, repair).

## How it runs

**Option:** packets

- **Start.** See [Toolkit check at start](#toolkit-check-at-start).
- **Dispatch.** The orchestrator marks a unit `READY <unit>` on the board when its Depends are merged. The manager then writes that unit's packet from its cards, into the [chat start message](#start-messages): outcome, May touch (the card's Scope), focused proof, base SHA and the target `release/<N>/plan-<PP>`. No packet files are prepared ahead. The older `releases/16/packets/R16-P66-*`, `-P67-*`, `-P68-*` and `-P70-*` files are history only.
- **Authors (ChatGPT route).**
  - **The chats.** One fresh normal ChatGPT chat on the web per unit, signed in to the owner's own account, in the unit's route mode: N High, X Extra High, S Pro ([modes](../../../agent/shared/run-contract.md#chatgpt-session-modes)). Never ChatGPT for Work, and never one mode for everything. T editorial work goes to a fresh Claude Opus session. The chat writes the unit, runs its focused proof in its sandbox, pushes through its GitHub connector, and posts its early STATUS, READY-PROOF or BLOCKED on the lane board.
  - **The manager** (`h3-manager`) is one Codex session in the Codex app on the owner's computer, with Chrome signed in to ChatGPT. It opens, names and resumes the chats, writes each Ready unit's packet, and keeps at most three chats running. It does not run proof, merge or rule; it relays those to the orchestrator on the board. It runs on Terra (route X), checks in hourly at a minute other than :17 and :30, and may replace a chat once (branch quiet 30 minutes, the head named in the new chat's first message). A second stall parks the unit for the owner. The orchestrator cannot create or wake it, so:
    1. At start, and for every replacement, the orchestrator posts the [manager launch](#start-messages) on the lane board as `LAUNCH h3-manager` and tells the owner in its STATUS.
    2. The owner pastes it into a new Codex thread. Nobody else sends it.
    3. The manager follows the recorded Codex-app steps on [#2030](https://github.com/banodoco/astrid-dev/issues/2030) (`TOOLKIT` comments): its hourly check-in automation, opening a chat and choosing its mode, and resuming a chat. #2030 records only High. The first time it chooses Extra High or Pro, it posts the exact steps as a `TOOLKIT` comment on the lane board. If a mode isn't offered, it posts BLOCKED and does not substitute another.
  - **The orchestrator** runs the queue: it marks units Ready on the board, answers each READY-PROOF, sends reviews, merges and replaces sessions.
  - **Fallback, at a unit boundary, said once on the board:** if no `h3-manager` STATUS appears within two hours of a LAUNCH post, or the manager is silent for three hours ([liveness](../../../agent/shared/run-contract.md#coordination-boards-and-check-ins)), the orchestrator runs units itself as Codex workers (`codex exec`, the [guide](../../../agent/guides/codex-in-claude-cloud/README.md#use-codex-for-the-workers-under-a-claude-manager); N Luna, X Terra, S Sol; the owner opens one `codex login --device-auth` link). Codex not yet installed or signed in is not unavailable: it installs it and posts the sign-in link and code in its own reply, telling the owner on the board. A sign-in the owner hasn't completed by the next check-in counts as failed. Only if the sign-in or a model ID then fails does it use Claude workers by the same routes (N Haiku, X Sonnet, S Opus). It returns to the chats when a manager posts again.
- **Baseline.** Before 66-U1 (whose Depends says `baseline`), the orchestrator records the test inventory on the starting versions: the hosted CI result on each plan branch at its cut SHA, starting one run per branch if none exists ([when a run is load-bearing](../../../agent/shared/run-contract.md#when-a-test-run-is-load-bearing)). It posts `BASELINE <plan> <sha> <run link>` on the board. That post is the "refreshed 59/64/65 baseline" Plan 66's critical path starts from, and it satisfies `baseline`. Review findings take the IDs their card names (for example `RS1.<n>`).
- **Proof.** The author commits its own work. A Codex worker or subagent returns the SHA, and the orchestrator pushes and opens the PR; a chat or worker session pushes `p<PP>/<unit>` and opens a draft PR into the plan branch, marking it ready when it posts READY-PROOF. It runs the focused proof in its sandbox only if it can without cloning or building. H3 tests need [environment setup](../../../env/README.md) that fetches other repositories, so usually it can't, and says so. The evidence is then the PR's green CI on the plan branch: the orchestrator confirms the PR head equals the posted SHA, and that the CI run on that head ran the unit's focused tests by id (every one of its cards' Focused proof lists). Once Plan 66 CC-14 lands, its `engine_deps` lane is the one proof workflow for every unit and pack. A unit may land as several PRs. There is no separate proof custodian. A Claude or Codex worker on the fallback can build: it sets up the environment with the repository's `scripts/worker-env/` and runs the focused tests before READY-PROOF.
- **Reviews.**
  - The reviewer's ACCEPT is required at Plan 66 RS1 (job-record half, then contract half), on every S-route unit, at Plan 67 R (the programme's integrated review) and at Plan 107 R. Other units merge on green CI and shown proof.
  - An S-route unit's own review gates that unit's PR; a review stop after it (66-RS1a after 66-U1) is a separate review of the merged head. Findings become rows under the reviewed card: `RS1.<n>` at the RS1 stops, `R1.<m>` for a unit's own review, under its first card. They go back to the same author once, then to a fresh session.
- **Merges.**
  - The orchestrator merges `release/<N>/main` forward into each plan branch as it moves.
  - When a plan's units are merged, and its review stop accepted where it has one, it merges the plan branch into `release/<N>/main`. It does this after any overlapping merge from other lanes. This is the lane's release gate: the owner's go above covers these merges, and no Lead gate applies.
  - The orchestrator also merges its own astrid-dev progress PRs on a green check, when they touch only this lane's plan status lines, `tasks.md` rows and this brief.
  - It cuts later branches itself with `git push` from the base named in the release brief.
- **CI.** astrid-agent CI is shared with Plans 93, 94 and 38:
  - batch several units per push;
  - keep one run per branch;
  - cancel your own superseded runs;
  - check the running `ci.yml` runs before pushing;
  - keep heavy suites on CI, never on a shared session.
- **Check-ins.**
  - The orchestrator creates its hourly scheduled task (`create_trigger` on this session) at start, without waiting to be asked, and confirms its next run. That task is its wake path; it needs no separate keep-awake loop. The fallback clock starts at the LAUNCH post. The manager keeps its own hourly Codex automation. Chats only post milestones. Each check reads the lane board by cursor (`python3 agent/guides/codex-cloud/inbox.py banodoco/astrid-dev <board number> --after <cursor>`), acts, and posts one STATUS of 10 lines or fewer: units merged, units running, blocked items with their cause, the next units, `acted on:` and `cursor:`.
  - Anyone writes to the orchestrator by posting on the lane board with `to: h3-orchestrator` and a type (STATUS, READY-PROOF, REQUEST, DECISION, BLOCKED); the manager's hourly STATUS and every chat's milestones go `to: h3-orchestrator`; the format is in the board's first post, copied from the [mailbox format](../../../agent/guides/codex-cloud/README.md#establish-the-mailbox) with `run: h3-lane`. It answers in the next STATUS or in a reply there.
  - Something that cannot wait an hour: a Claude session also sends `send_message` to the orchestrator's session id (in this brief's header) as a doorbell, after posting on the board. The owner can also write in the orchestrator's session directly. Nothing can wake the Codex manager or a chat: they read the board at their next check-in, and an urgent message to them goes to the owner.
  - The reviewer posts its verdicts on the lane board. The orchestrator posts to the Release 16 Lead only on the Lead's board, and only for release-level questions.
- **Skills** are hand-written editorial guides, mixing instruction and creative inspiration, and never generated. Plan 66 CC-6 and Plan 67 CS-8 write them; each pack ships its own written section.
